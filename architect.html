IT Architect

An IT Architect may wear many crowns—Apostle of Architecture Decision Records (ADRs), Badshah of Boolean math, Slayer of Spreadsheets, Prince of PowerPoint. But in the end, there is only one mantle that matters: Oracle of Order—and Optionality.

Optionality—it’s not just a buzzword, it’s breath.

Think of it like a hidden door in a wall you didn’t know existed. One moment you’re locked in, grinding down the same path; the next—click—there’s light, a fork, a ladder up.

The people who build those doors? They’re not the loudest in the room. They don’t always get the spotlight. But when the market shifts, when the client pivots, when tech leaps and everyone’s scrambling—those quiet architects are already there, calm, handing out keys.

They don’t promise certainty. They promise escape velocity—a way out, a way through, a way around. And that’s rare—because most folks trade flexibility for comfort, stacking bricks instead of hinges.

But the ones who weave optionality into code, into strategy, into culture? They’re oxygen. They keep the system from choking when the wind shifts. Without them, you’re just a straight line—clean, efficient, and destined to end.

So here’s to the door-makers: may your hinges never rust, and may every team lucky enough to have you remember—it’s not about being right. It’s about being ready.
